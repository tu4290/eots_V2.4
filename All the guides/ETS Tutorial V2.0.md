The Elite Options Trading System is built upon the philosophy that inefficiencies and predictable patterns arise from the hedging activities of market makers and significant options order flow. ## II. Core Concepts & Terminology

Understanding the following terms is essential for effectively using the system:

-   **Options Greeks**: These measure the sensitivity of an option's price to various factors.
    -   **Delta**: Measures the rate of change of an option's price relative to a $1 change in the underlying asset's price. Ranges from 0 to 1 for calls, -1 to 0 for puts.
    -   **Gamma**: Measures the rate of change of an option's Delta relative to a $1 change in the underlying asset's price. Highest for at-the-money options near expiration.
    -   **Theta**: Measures the rate of change of an option's price relative to the passage of time (time decay). Generally negative for long options.
    -   **Vega**: Measures the rate of change of an option's price relative to a 1% change in the implied volatility of the underlying asset.
    -   **Charm (Delta Decay)**: Measures the rate of change of an option's Delta with respect to the passage of time.
    -   **Vanna**: Measures the rate of change of an option's Delta with respect to a change in implied volatility. Also, the rate of change of Vega with respect to a change in the underlying price.
    -   **Vomma**: Measures the rate of change of an option's Vega with respect to a change in implied volatility.
-   **Open Interest (OI)**: The total number of outstanding options contracts that have not been settled or closed. High OI at a strike can indicate significance.
-   **Volume**: The total number of options contracts traded during a given period. High volume indicates activity and interest at certain strikes.
-   **GEX (Gamma Exposure)**: The total gamma sensitivity across all options at a particular strike price or for the entire market. Positive GEX tends to suppress volatility (dealers hedge by buying into dips and selling into rallies), while negative GEX (less common for broad indices) can exacerbate it. (Knowledge: User_1)
    -   **Skew-Adjusted GEX**: GEX that accounts for volatility skew, providing a more realistic measure of hedging impact. (Knowledge: User_6, User_8)
-   **DEX (Delta Exposure)**: The total delta sensitivity at a strike or for the market. Indicates the net directional hedging required by market makers.
-   **Volatility Skew**: The difference in implied volatility (IV) between out-of-the-money (OTM), at-the-money (ATM), and in-the-money (ITM) options. Typically, OTM puts have higher IV than OTM calls for equities and indices.
-   **"Gamma Wall"**: A price level with a very large concentration of GEX, acting as strong support or resistance, often like a price magnet. (Knowledge: User_1, User_35, User_38, User_43)
-   **"Volatility Trigger"**: A price level, often identified by strongly negative SDAG values, where market volatility dynamics are expected to change significantly. Breaching these levels can lead to accelerated moves. (Knowledge: User_33, User_15, User_19)
-   **Market Maker Hedging**: Options market makers aim to remain delta-neutral. As the underlying price moves, they must buy or sell the underlying asset to offset the changing delta of their options book. This hedging activity can influence price, especially around large GEX strikes.
-   **Order Flow Imbalance (OFI)**: The net difference between buying and selling pressure in the options market, often measured by volume or value of aggressive buy orders versus sell orders.


## III. Individual Metrics Explained

This section delves into the individual metrics calculated by the system. Each metric provides a unique lens through which to analyze market structure and potential price movements. Understanding each one is crucial before attempting to synthesize them into a cohesive trading strategy.

### 1. Delta Adjusted Gamma Exposure (DAG - Custom)

- **Metric Name & Abbreviation**: Delta Adjusted Gamma Exposure (DAG - Custom)
- **Conceptual Explanation**: The custom DAG metric is a core component of the MSPI. It aims to measure the combined influence of gamma and delta exposures at various strike prices. Unlike raw GEX (Gamma Exposure) which only shows the magnitude of potential hedging, DAG incorporates the directional bias of Delta Exposure (DEX). This provides a more nuanced view of how market maker hedging might influence price, especially around significant strike levels. It tries to identify points where dealer hedging could either accelerate a move (if delta and gamma align) or dampen it (if they oppose).
- **Simplified Calculation Insight**: The custom DAG is calculated within the `IntegratedTradingSystem` by considering both the `gamma_exposure_source_col` (typically `gxoi`) and `delta_exposure_source_col` (typically `dxoi`) from the input options data. The exact proprietary calculation logic within the `_calculate_dag` method of the original `integrated_strategies.py` (which `integrated_strategies_v2.py` builds upon) combines these, potentially applying coefficients based on their alignment (e.g., `dag_alpha` from `config_v2.json` which has `aligned`, `opposed`, `neutral` factors).
- **Visual Representation**: [Placeholder for DAG chart - typically a bar chart showing DAG values per strike price, similar to GEX/DEX charts but representing the combined metric].
- **Interpretation Guide**:
    - High positive DAG values at a strike might suggest a level where market maker hedging could support price or accelerate a move upwards if price is already moving towards it.
    - High negative DAG values (depending on how it's scaled, or if it represents net exposure) could indicate resistance or areas where hedging might accelerate a downward move.
    - The distribution of DAG across strikes helps identify key inflection points.
    - The interpretation is heavily influenced by the `dag_alpha` coefficients in `config_v2.json -> data_processor_settings -> coefficients`. If `aligned` is > 1, it amplifies the effect when delta and gamma suggest the same hedging pressure.
- **Practical Use Cases & Examples**:
    - Identifying strong support/resistance levels based on combined dealer positioning.
    - Gauging the potential for price to be "pinned" to or repelled from certain strikes due to hedging flows.
- **Relationship to Other Metrics**: DAG is a primary input into the Market Structure Position Indicator (MSPI). It's also conceptually related to the various SDAG methodologies, which offer alternative ways to combine gamma and delta, often with skew adjustment.
- **Configuration Notes**:
    - `data_processor_settings.weights.*.*.dag_custom`: Weight of this custom DAG in the MSPI calculation, varies by time or IV context.
    - `data_processor_settings.coefficients.dag_alpha`: Modifies DAG based on delta/gamma alignment.
    - `strategy_settings.gamma_exposure_source_col`: Source column for gamma (e.g., "gxoi").
    - `strategy_settings.delta_exposure_source_col`: Source column for delta (e.g., "dxoi").

### 2. Skew and Delta Adjusted GEX (SDAG) Methodologies

The system implements several SDAG methodologies to provide a more refined view of market pressures by potentially incorporating volatility skew.

- **Metric Name & Abbreviation**: Skew and Delta Adjusted GEX (SDAG)
    - SDAG Multiplicative
    - SDAG Directional
    - SDAG Weighted
    - SDAG Volatility-Focused
- **Conceptual Explanation**: SDAG metrics build upon the concept of DAG by explicitly accounting for volatility skew (if `use_skew_adjusted_for_sdag` is true and `skew_adjusted_gamma_source_col` data is available) and then combining this with delta exposure. The goal is to get an even more accurate picture of true hedging pressures. Different methodologies combine these factors in slightly different ways, offering various perspectives.
    - **Knowledge User_10, User_11, User_15, User_16, User_19, User_21, User_22**: These knowledge items emphasize that SDAG provides a more comprehensive view than GEX or DEX alone, helps identify precise support/resistance, and that alignment across methodologies gives higher conviction.
- **Simplified Calculation Insight**:
    - Each SDAG methodology (`multiplicative`, `directional`, `weighted`, `volatility_focused`) has its own formula for combining gamma (potentially skew-adjusted) and delta. These are implemented in `_calculate_sdag_multiplicative`, `_calculate_sdag_directional`, `_calculate_sdag_weighted`, and `_calculate_sdag_volatility_focused` methods in `integrated_strategies_v2.py`.
    - **Multiplicative**: `Gamma_Component * (1 + Delta_Component * delta_weight_factor)`
    - **Directional**: `Gamma_Component * sign(Gamma_Component * Delta_Component) * (1 + abs(Delta_Component * delta_weight_factor))` (Note: `delta_weight_factor` is applied to Delta before abs in current code, effectively `(1 + abs(Delta_Component) * delta_weight_factor)` if `delta_weight_factor` is from config for the method, or just `(1 + abs(Delta_Component))` if `delta_weight_factor` is not used as a multiplier for delta component itself in the formula. The `integrated_strategies_v2.py` code for `_calculate_sdag_directional` uses `(1 + delta_component.abs() * methodology_config.get("delta_weight_factor", 0.5))` which seems to be a slight variation from pure `(1 + |Delta|)` if `delta_weight_factor` is not 1. The knowledge base `user_21` and `user_22` suggests `(1 + |Delta|)` or `(1 + Delta * sign(Skew_Adjusted_GEX))` for Volatility-Focused. The code uses `delta_weight_factor` from config for most methods.
    - **Weighted**: `(w1_gamma * Gamma_Component + w2_delta * Delta_Component) / (w1_gamma + w2_delta)`
    - **Volatility-Focused**: `Gamma_Component * (1 + Delta_Component * sign(Gamma_Component) * delta_weight_factor)` (Code uses `delta_weight_factor` from config).
    - The `Gamma_Component` is either standard GEX (e.g., `gxoi`) or Skew-Adjusted GEX (e.g., `sgxoi`) based on `use_skew_adjusted_for_sdag`.
    - **Knowledge User_21, User_22**: Provide specific formulas for these methodologies.
- **Visual Representation**: [Placeholder for SDAG charts - typically line or bar charts per methodology, showing values per strike. A combined chart showing alignment is also powerful].
- **Interpretation Guide**:
    - **Knowledge User_15, User_16, User_19**: SDAG values > 1.5 indicate extremely strong positive signals (support/resistance), while < -1.5 are extremely strong negative signals (volatility triggers).
    - **Knowledge User_19**: Provides detailed ranges: `>1.5` (extremely strong positive), `1.0-1.5` (strong positive), `0.5-1.0` (moderate positive), etc.
    - Declining slopes from peaks indicate decreasing stability.
    - **Knowledge User_10, User_15, User_16, User_21, User_22**: Alignment of multiple (or all four) SDAG methodologies at specific price levels provides the highest conviction trading signals.
    - The `sdag_conviction` signal is generated when a configurable majority of enabled SDAG methodologies agree.
- **Practical Use Cases & Examples**:
    - Identifying high-conviction support and resistance levels.
    - Pinpointing potential volatility trigger points.
    - Range trading between key SDAG levels.
    - **Knowledge User_21, User_22**: Suggests focusing on Multiplicative/Directional for day trading, Weighted for swing trading, and Volatility-Focused for options strategies.
- **Relationship to Other Metrics**: Enabled SDAG methodologies (if their `weight_in_mspi` > 0) are normalized and then included in the MSPI calculation. They provide an alternative or supplementary view to the custom DAG.
- **Configuration Notes**:
    - `strategy_settings.gamma_exposure_source_col`, `strategy_settings.delta_exposure_source_col`, `strategy_settings.skew_adjusted_gamma_source_col`.
    - `strategy_settings.use_skew_adjusted_for_sdag`: Boolean to use skew-adjusted GEX for SDAGs.
    - `strategy_settings.dag_methodologies.enabled`: List of which SDAGs to calculate.
    - `strategy_settings.dag_methodologies.[method_name]`: Specific parameters for each SDAG (e.g., `delta_weight_factor`, `w1_gamma`, `w2_delta`, `weight_in_mspi`).
    - `strategy_settings.sdag_conviction_threshold_pct`: Percentage of enabled SDAGs that must agree for a conviction signal.
    - `data_processor_settings.weights.*.*.sdag_[method_name]_norm`: Weights of normalized SDAGs in MSPI if `weight_in_mspi` for the method is > 0.

It considers how accelerating time decay might force hedging adjustments or create pinning effects around strikes with large open interest, especially those near the current underlying price.

It typically uses a Gaussian function centered around the current underlying price, weighted by open interest or gamma at different strikes. The width of this Gaussian (`tdpi_gaussian_width`) determines how far out the "pressure" is felt. It also incorporates an ATR (Average True Range) concept, potentially as a fallback or scaling factor (`tdpi_atr_fallback`). ### 6. Sentiment Alignment Indicator (SAI)

- **Metric Name & Abbreviation**: Sentiment Alignment Indicator (SAI)
- **Conceptual Explanation**: SAI measures the degree of alignment or divergence between the MSPI (representing overall market structure) and short-term order flow dynamics (represented by OFI - Order Flow Imbalance). It helps identify if current buying/selling pressure is confirming or contradicting the underlying market structure.
- **Simplified Calculation Insight**: SAI is calculated in `_calculate_sai`. It essentially compares MSPI (or a derivative) with OFI. A positive SAI indicates alignment (e.g., bullish structure and bullish flow), while a negative SAI indicates divergence (e.g., bullish structure but bearish flow).
- **Visual Representation**: [Placeholder for SAI chart - often a histogram showing positive/negative bars, indicating alignment or divergence].
- **Interpretation Guide**:
    - Strong positive SAI: High conviction that short-term flow supports the market structure. E.g., if MSPI is bullish at a level, and OFI is also bullish, SAI will be strongly positive.
    - Strong negative SAI: High conviction that short-term flow opposes the market structure. This could signal a potential weakening of the structure or a short-term counter-trend move.
    - Values around zero: Neutral or mixed signals from flow relative to structure.
    - The `sai_high_conviction` threshold in config determines what's considered a strong signal.
- **Practical Use Cases & Examples**:

## V. Cohesive Analysis: Integrating Metrics & Signals

Understanding individual metrics and signals is the foundation, but the true power of this trading system comes from synthesizing this information into a cohesive market view. This section focuses on how to combine various components to make more informed trading decisions.

### 1. The Big Picture: How Individual Components Work Together

The system is designed around a core idea: market maker hedging and significant order flow can create predictable (or at least probabilistically favorable) patterns and levels in the market. 

- **Structural Foundation (MSPI & its components - DAG, SDAGs, TDPI, VRI)**: These metrics establish the underlying market structure. High MSPI/SDAG values at specific strikes suggest strong support/resistance due to concentrated dealer positions. TDPI highlights time-decay pressures, especially near expiration, while VRI assesses the volatility environment.
- **Flow Confirmation/Divergence (SAI, CFI, OFI)**: These metrics overlay short-term and cumulative order flow onto the established structure. Positive SAI (alignment) strengthens the conviction in MSPI levels, while negative SAI (divergence) can signal caution or potential turning points. CFI tracks sustained pressure.
- **Stability Assessment (SSI)**: SSI tells you how robust the current structure is. A stable structure (high SSI) might favor range-bound strategies around key MSPI/SDAG levels. A less stable structure (low SSI) might suggest caution or preparedness for breaks.
- **Specific Event Triggers (Volatility Signals, Pin Risk, Charm Cascade, Structure Change, Flow Divergence)**: These are discrete signals generated when specific thresholds (defined in `config_v2.json`) are met by the underlying metrics. They act as alerts for potentially actionable conditions.

**The Goal**: To find confluence. When multiple indicators point in the same direction, the conviction level for a trading idea increases. For example:
- Strong MSPI/SDAG support level + Positive SAI + High SSI + Bullish directional signal = Higher conviction long entry.
- Strong MSPI/SDAG resistance level + Negative SAI (flow diverging) + Low SSI + Bearish signal = Potential short opportunity or caution for longs.

## VII. Advanced Configuration & Customization

This section provides a deeper look into `config_v2.json`, enabling advanced users to tailor the system's behavior to their specific needs and market views. Always back up your `config_v2.json` before making significant changes.

### 1. Deep Dive into `config_v2.json` Sections

(Refer to your `config_v2.json` and `config.schema.json` for the full structure. Key areas for trading logic customization are highlighted below.)

-   **`data_processor_settings.weights`**: 
    -   `selection_logic`: Choose between "time_based" (weights change for morning, midday, final hours) or "volatility_based" (weights change based on IV percentile).
    -   `time_based` & `volatility_based` sub-sections: Define the actual percentage weights for `dag_custom`, `tdpi`, `vri`, and each enabled `sdag_[method_name]_norm` that contributes to MSPI. The sum of these weights for any given period/context should ideally be close to 1.0.
    -   *Impact*: Directly alters the composition and sensitivity of the MSPI. If you believe TDPI is more critical mid-day, you can increase its weight for that period.

-   **`data_processor_settings.coefficients`**: (e.g., `dag_alpha`, `tdpi_beta`, `vri_gamma`)
    -   These apply multipliers to the respective metrics based on whether their internal components are "aligned", "opposed", or "neutral".
    -   *Impact*: Allows you to amplify or dampen the effect of a metric when its underlying drivers are strongly confirming or conflicting. For example, a `dag_alpha.aligned` of 1.3 boosts DAG when its gamma and delta components suggest similar hedging pressure.

-   **`data_processor_settings.factors`**: (e.g., `tdpi_gaussian_width`, `vri_vol_trend_fallback_factor`)
    -   Fine-tunes specific aspects of metric calculations.
    -   `tdpi_gaussian_width`: A negative value (e.g., -0.5) typically makes the Gaussian curve wider, meaning TDPI considers strikes further from the current price. A value closer to zero would make it narrower, focusing pressure more tightly.
    -   *Impact*: Modifies the shape and reach of individual metric calculations.

-   **`strategy_settings.gamma_exposure_source_col`, `delta_exposure_source_col`, `skew_adjusted_gamma_source_col`**: 
    -   Defines which columns from your input data are used for GEX, DEX, and Skew-Adjusted GEX.
    -   *Impact*: Critical for ensuring the system uses the correct data. `gxoi` (gamma * open interest) and `dxoi` (delta * open interest) are common. If you have skew-adjusted GEX data, specify its column name and set `use_skew_adjusted_for_sdag` to true.

-   **`strategy_settings.use_skew_adjusted_for_sdag`**: 
    -   Boolean (true/false). If true, SDAG calculations will use the `skew_adjusted_gamma_source_col`. If false, they use the standard `gamma_exposure_source_col`.
    -   *Impact*: Determines if SDAG methodologies incorporate volatility skew, which can provide a more realistic view of hedging pressures (Knowledge: User_6, User_8).

-   **`strategy_settings.dag_methodologies`**: 
    -   `enabled`: A list of SDAG methodologies to calculate (e.g., `["multiplicative", "directional"]`).
    -   For each methodology (e.g., `multiplicative`): 
        -   `delta_weight_factor` (or `w1_gamma`, `w2_delta` for weighted): Parameters specific to that SDAG formula.
        -   `weight_in_mspi`: How much this normalized SDAG methodology contributes to the overall MSPI score. If 0, it's calculated for analysis/signals but not part of MSPI.
    -   *Impact*: Controls which advanced SDAG views are available and how they influence the composite MSPI.

-   **`strategy_settings.sdag_conviction_threshold_pct`**: 
    -   Percentage (0.0 to 1.0). The proportion of *enabled* SDAG methodologies that must agree on a sentiment (bullish/bearish at a strike) to generate an `sdag_conviction` signal.
    -   *Impact*: A higher value (e.g., 0.75 meaning 3 out of 4 enabled must agree) leads to fewer but higher-conviction SDAG agreement signals.

-   **`strategy_settings.thresholds`**: 
    -   This is a large section defining trigger points for various signals (SAI, SSI, CFI, Volatility Expansion/Contraction, Pin Risk, Charm Cascade).
    -   Each threshold has a `type` (`fixed`, `relative_percentile`, `relative_mean_factor`) and a `value` (or `percentile`, `factor`). Many also have a `fallback_value`.
    -   *Impact*: Directly controls the sensitivity of all discrete trading signals. Adjusting these requires careful consideration and testing, as it changes how readily the system will alert you to specific conditions.
        -   Example: `sai_high_conviction.value`: If 0.7, SAI must be >0.7 or <-0.7 for a high conviction signal.
        -   Example: `ssi_structure_change.percentile`: If 15, an SSI value in the bottom 15th percentile of its recent range will trigger a structure change warning.

### 2. How to Adjust Parameters for Different Market Conditions or Risk Appetites

-   **Volatile Markets**: 
    -   Consider increasing the weight of VRI in MSPI.
    -   Lower thresholds for Volatility Expansion signals to be alerted sooner.
    -   Potentially rely more on shorter-term SDAG methodologies (Multiplicative, Directional).
-   **Quiet/Ranging Markets**: 
    -   Increase weight of TDPI if near expiration.
    -   Focus on SDAG levels for range boundaries.
    -   Adjust thresholds for CFI/SAI to catch divergences that might signal a range break.
-   **Higher Risk Appetite**: 
    -   May lower conviction thresholds slightly to get more signals, understanding this may increase false positives.
-   **Lower Risk Appetite**: 
    -   Increase conviction thresholds (e.g., `sai_high_conviction`, `sdag_conviction_threshold_pct`).
    -   Require stronger alignment across multiple metrics before acting.

### 3. Understanding the Impact of Configuration Changes

-   **Test Incrementally**: Change one group of related parameters at a time and observe the impact on historical data or a paper trading account before applying to live trading.
-   **Normalization is Key**: Remember that MSPI components (DAG, TDPI, VRI, SDAGs) are normalized before being weighted. Changing the calculation of one component (e.g., via its factors or coefficients) can affect its normalized range and thus its influence on MSPI, even if its weight remains the same.
-   **Documentation is Crucial**: Keep a personal log of changes made to `config_v2.json` and the observed outcomes. This will help you refine your configuration over time.

By mastering the configuration, you transform the system from a generic tool into a personalized trading assistant that reflects your market understanding and risk preferences.

-   **Q1: The dashboard is not loading data or shows errors.**

        -   Check `config_v2.json`: Ensure `api_credentials` (email, password, environment) for ConvexValue are correctly entered if you intend to fetch live data. If not, the system will use dummy data for the dashboard if `enhanced_data_fetcher_v2.py` is set up to do so.

## IX. Glossary of All Metrics & Signals

-   **CFI (Cumulative Flow Imbalance)**: Tracks net cumulative order flow over time.
-   **CTR (Charm Decay Rate)**: Measures the rate of delta decay due to charm (time passage effect on delta).
-   **DAG (Delta Adjusted Gamma Exposure - Custom)**: Proprietary metric combining gamma and delta exposures to identify key structural levels.
-   **DEX (Delta Exposure)**: Net delta sensitivity of options positions.
-   **GEX (Gamma Exposure)**: Net gamma sensitivity of options positions.
-   **MSPI (Market Structure Position Indicator)**: Primary composite indicator synthesizing DAG, TDPI, VRI, and SDAGs.
-   **OFI (Order Flow Imbalance)**: Net short-term buying or selling pressure from options order flow.
-   **SAI (Sentiment Alignment Indicator)**: Measures alignment between MSPI (structure) and OFI (flow).
-   **SDAG (Skew and Delta Adjusted GEX)**: Advanced metric combining (skew-adjusted) GEX with DEX. Multiple methodologies exist (Multiplicative, Directional, Weighted, Volatility-Focused).
-   **SSI (Structural Stability Index)**: Assesses the stability of the current market structure.
-   **TDFI (Time Decay Flow Imbalance)**: Net flow into options highly sensitive to time decay.
-   **TDPI (Time Decay Pressure Indicator)**: Measures market impact of time decay, especially near expiration.
-   **VFI (Volatility Flow Imbalance)**: Net flow into options sensitive to volatility changes.
-   **VRI (Volatility Risk Indicator)**: Quantifies risks/opportunities from changes in implied/realized volatility.

-   **Directional Signal**: Bullish/Bearish/Neutral signal based on MSPI and SAI.
-   **SDAG Conviction Signal**: Bullish/Bearish signal based on agreement among multiple SDAG methodologies.
-   **Volatility Expansion Signal**: Indicates conditions favoring an increase in market volatility.
-   **Volatility Contraction Signal**: Indicates conditions favoring a decrease in market volatility.
-   **Time Decay Pin Risk Signal**: High probability of price pinning to a strike due to time decay.
-   **Time Decay Charm Cascade Signal**: Potential for accelerated moves due to charm-induced delta decay hedging.
-   **Complex Structure Change Signal**: Warning that the current market structure may be shifting.
-   **Complex Flow Divergence Signal**: Alert for divergences between price and cumulative order flow.
